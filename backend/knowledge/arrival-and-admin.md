# Arrival and practical admin for international students

> Curated general guidance for the ShefGuide prototype. This is the project's
> own knowledge base, not an official University of Sheffield publication.
> Immigration and healthcare rules change often and depend on individual
> circumstances. ShefGuide does not give immigration, financial, or medical
> advice; students must confirm details with the university's international
> student support team or the relevant official service.

## Registering with a GP

A GP (general practitioner) is the usual first point of contact for
non-emergency healthcare in the UK. Registration is normally free and is done
at a local practice near where you live. Registering before you are ill is
sensible, because appointments can take time to arrange.

For urgent but non-emergency problems the NHS 111 service can advise. In an
emergency the number is 999.

## Opening a bank account

A UK bank account is commonly needed for paying rent and receiving funds.
Banks typically ask for identification, evidence of your student status, and
proof of your UK address. Universities can usually issue a bank letter or
confirmation of study for this purpose. Requirements vary between banks, and
some offer accounts designed for international students.

## University IT and email

Activating your university account early matters because module materials,
reading lists, timetables, submission systems, and marks are usually only
accessible through it. Many institutions also provide a VPN for off-campus
access to licensed library resources.

## Student ID card

The student card is typically needed for building access, library borrowing,
examinations, and student discounts.

## Council tax

Full-time students are generally exempt from council tax, but the exemption
usually has to be claimed with evidence of enrolment rather than applied
automatically. The rules differ for shared households where not everyone is
a student.

## Accommodation basics

For private rentals, a tenancy agreement is a binding contract and should be
read before signing. Deposits for assured shorthold tenancies in England must
normally be protected in a government-approved deposit protection scheme, and
the tenant should receive confirmation of which scheme holds it. University
accommodation services and students' union advice centres commonly review
contracts for students at no charge.

## Where to go for what

Academic questions go to the personal tutor or module leader. Practical and
immigration questions go to the international student support team. Wellbeing
and mental health questions go to student services or the students' union
advice centre. ShefGuide redirects to these services rather than attempting
to answer such questions itself.
