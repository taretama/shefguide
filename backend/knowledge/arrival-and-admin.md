# Arrival and practical admin for international students

> Curated guidance for the ShefGuide prototype, grounded in UKCISA's
> published student advice (see Provenance below). UKCISA is the UK's
> national advisory body for international student affairs. Immigration and
> healthcare rules change often and depend on individual circumstances.
> ShefGuide does not give immigration, financial, or medical advice;
> students must confirm details with the university's international student
> support team or the relevant official service.

## Immigration status: eVisas have replaced the BRP

Since 31 October 2024, the Home Office has stopped issuing physical
Biometric Residence Permits (BRPs) for new Student visa holders. Immigration
status is now held digitally as an eVisa, viewable through a UKVI account,
rather than collected as a card. Students who still hold an older physical
BRP from before the change should check UKCISA's eVisa guidance for what,
if anything, they need to do (UKCISA, "eVisas").

## Registering with a GP

A GP (general practitioner) is the usual first point of contact for
non-emergency healthcare in the UK, and registration is normally free.
UKCISA's guidance recommends registering as soon as possible after arrival
rather than waiting until you are unwell, since registration has to happen
before an appointment can be booked. Doctors in England, Wales, and Scotland
normally accept students on courses of any duration; in Northern Ireland the
course must normally last at least six months. To register, bring a letter
from your institution confirming your student status, your passport, and
any immigration documents (UKCISA, "International students' healthcare").

For urgent but non-emergency problems the NHS 111 service can advise. In an
emergency the number is 999.

## Opening a bank account

International students typically need proof of identity, an immigration
status check, and evidence of their UK address to open an account. Many UK
universities issue a "Letter of Introduction for UK Banking Facilities",
which banks accept to help satisfy anti-money-laundering requirements —
this can substitute for some of the other paperwork a bank would otherwise
ask for. Banks are required to check that an applicant has valid permission
to remain in the UK, and some may be unable to open an account for a course
lasting six months or less (UKCISA, "Opening a bank account").

## University IT and email

Activating your university account early matters because module materials,
reading lists, timetables, submission systems, and marks are usually only
accessible through it. Many institutions also provide a VPN for off-campus
access to licensed library resources.

## Student ID card

The student card is typically needed for building access, library
borrowing, examinations, and student discounts.

## Council tax

Full-time students are generally exempt from council tax, but the
exemption usually has to be claimed with evidence of enrolment rather than
applied automatically. The rules differ for shared households where not
everyone is a student.

## Accommodation basics

For private rentals, a tenancy agreement is a binding contract and should
be read before signing. Deposits for assured shorthold tenancies in England
must normally be protected in a government-approved deposit protection
scheme, and the tenant should receive confirmation of which scheme holds
it. University accommodation services and students' union advice centres
commonly review contracts for students at no charge.

## Where to go for what

Academic questions go to the personal tutor or module leader. Practical and
immigration questions go to the international student support team, or
UKCISA directly for national-level immigration advice. Wellbeing and mental
health questions go to student services or the students' union advice
centre. ShefGuide redirects to these services rather than attempting to
answer such questions itself.
