# Referencing and citation

> Curated general guidance for the ShefGuide prototype. This is the project's
> own knowledge base, not an official University of Sheffield publication.
> Departments vary in the exact style required, so the module handbook or
> library referencing guide is authoritative.

## Why referencing matters

Referencing shows where an idea came from, lets a reader verify it, and
separates the student's own contribution from other people's work. Failure
to reference properly is treated as an academic offence even when there was
no intention to deceive.

## Harvard (author-date)

Harvard is an author-date system. A citation appears in the text as the
author surname and year, for example (Smith, 2020). Direct quotations
normally add a page number, for example (Smith, 2020, p. 45).

A full reference list appears at the end, ordered alphabetically by author
surname. A journal article entry typically includes author, year, article
title, journal name, volume, issue, and page range. A book entry typically
includes author, year, title, edition if relevant, place of publication,
and publisher.

Harvard has many local variants. Punctuation and italicisation differ
between institutions, so consistency with the department's own guide
matters more than any single online example.

## APA

APA is also author-date and is common in psychology and the social
sciences. In-text citations look similar to Harvard, for example
(Smith, 2020). The reference list is titled "References" and has specific
rules for capitalisation and for listing multiple authors, including the
use of an ampersand before the final author.

## IEEE

IEEE is a numeric style common in engineering and computer science. Sources
are numbered in the order they first appear, and cited in text as a bracketed
number such as [1]. The reference list is ordered by that number rather than
alphabetically.

## Paraphrasing

Paraphrasing means restating an idea in genuinely different wording and
sentence structure, and it still requires a citation. Changing a few words
of the original while keeping its structure is regarded as patchwriting and
is usually treated as poor academic practice or plagiarism.

## Reference managers

Tools such as Zotero, Mendeley, and EndNote store sources and generate
formatted citations. They reduce mechanical errors but do not guarantee
correctness: output should always be checked against the required style,
particularly for sources with unusual author or publication details.
